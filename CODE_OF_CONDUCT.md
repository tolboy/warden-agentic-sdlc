# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people can disagree with productively. Argue with the claim, not with the person
making it, and take "you are wrong about this" as information rather than as an attack.

This project is opinionated on purpose — [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) declines whole
categories of change before they are written, and [`spec/SPEC.md`](spec/SPEC.md) lists
invariants that a patch has to argue against rather than quietly break. That kind of project
only works if disagreement is cheap and personal. So: strong opinions about code, ordinary
courtesy about people.

## What is expected

- Assume the other person has a reason. Ask what it is before explaining why they are wrong.
- Bring evidence. A claim someone else can check is worth more than confidence, and this is
  the same standard the tool holds its own vendors to.
- Accept a decision you disagree with once it has been made and explained, or make the case
  again with something new. Re-litigating an argument unchanged is a way of wearing people
  down, not of persuading them.
- Say when you do not know. It costs nothing here.

## What is not

Harassment, sustained personal hostility, sexualised language or imagery, insults about who
somebody is rather than what they wrote, publishing private information, and deliberate
disruption of discussion.

None of that becomes acceptable because the technical point underneath it was correct.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and code review — anywhere
the project is what brought people together.

## Reporting

Write to **anatoly.ru@gmail.com**. Reports are read by the maintainer, kept confidential, and
answered. Say what happened and where; you do not have to argue that it was serious enough.

The maintainer decides what to do, which may be a private word, a request to change something,
removing a comment, or blocking an account. A report that turns out to be a misunderstanding is
still worth having made — that is what the address is for.

Reporting somebody in good faith will never be held against you. Retaliating against a reporter
is itself a violation, and a straightforward one.
